# Count-Aware Log-Normal K=1 Seed-42 Validation Screening

## Decision

The K=1 log-normal quantity head failed the frozen validation gate. Keep the
mark-free direct log-MSE model as the active Count-aware TitanTPP baseline. Do
not run K=1 multi-seed or held-out evaluation under the current shared-encoder
loss contract.

## Error Structure

K=1 improved TitanTPP MAE in the high-support body but lost the existing tail
advantage:

| Quantity stratum | log-MSE MAE | K=1 MAE | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `q <= p50` | 0.086813 | 0.077979 | improved |
| `p50 < q <= p90` | 0.993921 | 0.861975 | improved |
| `p90 < q <= p95` | 2.247319 | 1.367144 | improved |
| `p95 < q <= p99` | 2.794168 | 4.011816 | regressed |
| `q > p99` | 6.067983 | 10.624919 | regressed substantially |

The history breakdown shows the same tradeoff. K=1 improved `history <= 64`,
was effectively unchanged for `65-128`, and worsened `history > 128` MAE from
`0.143516` to `0.210794`.

## Failure Diagnosis

The selected TitanTPP K=1 checkpoint had quantity distribution NLL
`-2.158420` and Time NLL `0.351402`. The negative Gaussian density term made
the joint objective look favorable while the time model was severely damaged.
No TitanTPP K=1 epoch, including the minimum-MAE and minimum-Time-NLL epochs,
satisfied the frozen time safety threshold. Changing only checkpoint selection
cannot recover this candidate.

THP showed the same direction: K=1 worsened MAE by `12.0091%`, RMSE by
`52.1536%`, and Time NLL by `0.442550`. This indicates a head/loss and
shared-encoder gradient interaction rather than a Titan-specific encoder
failure.

## Conclusion

K=1 is a finite and executable negative ablation, but it is not an accepted
model enhancement. The direct log-MSE quantity head remains the baseline. A
future distribution-head experiment would need to separate or normalize the
quantity-density gradient before repeating this comparison; that is a new
contract and is not authorized by this result.
